? What is Automatic Frame Rate (AFR)?
? Do I really need this program?

! Motion video is always filmed at certain frame rate.
! Traditional movies are shot at 24 frames per second, TV broadcasting
! uses 30/60 (NTSC standard) or 25/50 (PAL and SECAM standards) frames
! per second.

! TV will display content at a certain frame rate, which is called
! "vertical refresh rate". Most TVs and displays can work at different
! refresh rates.

! When movie framerate is not a multiple of refresh rate, the picture
! will play perceptibly non-evenly, which is sometimes referred as
! "judder". This is especially perceived on scenes where camera
! slowly moves sideways (panning). For example, when playing a 24
! frames per second video on a TV which uses a 60 Hz refresh rate,
! the order of frames displayed on TV will be:

! 0 0 0 1 1 2 2 2 3 3 4 4 4 5 5 6 6 6 7 7 ...

! As you can see, frames are displayed for a different period of time:
! even frames are displayed three times (3/60 seconds) while odd frames
! are displayed just two times (2/60 seconds). This is what makes you
! feel uncomfortable while watching such content.

! AFR is the process of harmonization between video frame rate and
! vertical refresh rate. This doesn't always mean that refresh rate
! must be set exactly to video frame rate; it is only important that
! refresh rate must be a multiple of frame rate. For example, it is
! perfectly acceptable to watch 30 frames-per-second content on a
! 60Hz refresh rate display, because every frame will be displayed
! two times (2/60 seconds), which is same as 1/30 seconds you would
! get if refresh rate would be 30Hz.

? Should I disable "system" auto framerate when using AFRd?

! First of all, whether it works at all depends on the box: on some
! AmLogic-based devices it really does switch the mode, on others it is
! broken - either in kernel, or in Android itself. But if you use AFRd,
! it is still better to disable it: AFRd has many more settings (switch
! delays, fractional rate policy, blacklists of rates and applications),
! while the system AFR switches the mode its own way and ignores them.

! Second, when two mechanisms switch the rate at the same time, they
! conflict. In the better case you'll get longer "black screens" during
! refresh rate switches, and in worst case you can even get "black screen
! forever" until reboot.

! That's why I recommend disabling the system AFR when using AFRd:
! "Match content frame rate" in Android TV settings, as well as vendor
! frame rate switches in the box settings (on AmLogic it is usually
! called "Frame rate switch" / "auto frame rate switching").

! On Android 11 this matters especially: there AFRd switches the mode through
! the same vendor mechanism as the system AFR (the vendor SystemControl HAL) -
! two mechanisms going through the same node, so a conflict is more likely than
! on other versions.

! Auto framerate built into a video player is a separate case, see the
! next question.

? Should I enable auto framerate (AFR) in video players that have it,
? when using AFRd?

! Yes, it is better to keep it enabled. A player switches the refresh rate
! through the same system path as AFRd, and in practice this works
! consistently.

! For media files the player knows the frame rate from the metadata
! before playback starts and switches the mode in advance, while AFRd
! spends 10-20 seconds measuring it (see the question "Why does the mode
! switch not immediately..."). For streaming video the player has declared
! information about the frame rate (HLS/DASH manifest), which AFRd has no
! access to. Note that a player reports the *declared* rate instead of
! measuring it - a player's own estimate from the frame counter is not
! more accurate than ours (on IPTV we saw it overestimated by 2-4%).

! When both mechanisms are active, usually there is no conflict: if the
! player has already switched the mode to the one AFRd considers correct,
! AFRd does nothing. But if the targets differ (which is possible, for
! example, at the 24/25 fps boundary), AFRd will switch the mode to its
! own one 10-20 seconds later - you will see an extra "blink" in the
! middle of playback.

! So if you have made sure that the player handles AFR well on its own,
! add it to the "Players that AFR gets in the way of" row (it is on the top
! level of "Settings") - then AFRd will not switch the mode for that player
! and the extra "blink" will not happen.

! How to check: first disable the player's own AFR and see how the player
! behaves with AFRd alone, then enable the player's AFR and repeat. If the
! player switches the mode correctly on its own, add it to that list.
! Signs that the player's AFR and AFRd get in each other's way: the mode
! does not have time to settle, switches twice within one clip, or the
! screen stays in a "foreign" mode after playback stops.

! A caveat: only a player that really switches the rate itself should be
! put in that list. If the player has no AFR of its own, being on the list
! means that the system chooses the mode, so the system mode must not be
! lower than the content frame rate (usually 60Hz) - otherwise some videos
! will be "clamped" (see limitation 8 in README).

? I still see "judder" in videos. Why?
? Despite the fact that AFRd correctly switches refresh rate, I still
? see frame judder on playback.

! AFRd is not a magic pill, it will just switch display mode to one
! that is most suitable for current movie.

! Everything else depends on a lot of other factors - the quality of
! video stream, the quality of the hardware decoder, the program
! for playing video, the setup of your TV.

? Why does the mode switch not immediately, but 10-20 seconds after video start?

! The frame rate is detected from the hardware decoder frame counter. The counter
! can be overestimated (repeated frames at segment boundaries, AV1 decoder
! specifics), so a short measurement cannot tell 24 and 25 fps apart; the
! decision is taken over an observation window of several seconds.

! When two independent measurements agree, the switch happens earlier (about
! 14 seconds). The observation window is configurable in "Settings for
! Android 11 and above" ("Observation before switching", "Early decision").

! If you do not want to wait, on Android 11 and above there is a fast option -
! see the question "Can the mode be switched faster than in 10-20 seconds?".

? Can the mode be switched faster than in 10-20 seconds?

! Yes, on Android 11 and above: enable "Fast decision by declared frame rate"
! ("Settings" -> "Fine tuning (Android 11+)"). The mode is applied in 2-4 seconds
! from the frame rate the decoder declares - exactly how the built-in system AFR
! works: it does not measure the rate, it asks the decoder, which is why it is
! fast.

! The price is that the decision is provisional. Our own measurement keeps
! running, and if the declared rate falls into a different class (24/25, 30/60),
! the mode is corrected: you may see a second "blink" and a few seconds in a
! wrong mode, and the log gets the line "declared X Hz, measurement gave Y Hz -
! correcting the mode".

! On Android 10 and 11 this key is on by default (there it gives the behaviour
! of the system AFR), on Android 12-14 it is off and is enabled deliberately: on
! 5.x kernels the declared rate is sometimes garbage (on our test box an AV1
! clip at 23.976 is reported by the driver as "30 fps"), so the first switch may
! turn out wrong - the measurement will correct it.

! The measurement itself cannot be sped up: over a short window 24 and 25 frames
! per second are indistinguishable, so the fast decision relies on the declared
! value, not on measurement. You can only shorten the observation window
! ("Observation before switching" = 0 - the decision is taken from the first
! sub-window, about 7 seconds), which costs accuracy at the 24/25 boundary:
! 20 seconds is the measured optimum, at which 24 fps content is identified
! correctly in 86% of cases.

! On Android 6-9 there is nothing to wait for: the mode is switched right after
! video starts, and these settings are not available in the interface.

? How to make the screen blink less often during playback?

! Turn off "Restore mode on stop" - this is the analogue of the old "Stop delay"
! option: after video ends the mode is not restored, so there is no extra switch.
! Applications with their own frame rate matching keep working.

! "Keep mode while paused" is enabled by default: on pause the mode is kept, and
! on resume it is not switched again.

? Sometimes after switching the picture goes with jerks, as if 10-15 frames per second.

! This is a known peculiarity of mode switching: the system does not always
! re-configure synchronization for the new rate, and frames reach the display
! twice as rarely. The daemon checks delivery after switching and, if this
! happened, switches the mode once more (away and back); the log will contain
! a "delivery twice as rare" line.

! If the jerks persist, restart AFRd and, if it repeats, send the log.

? Green-ish/purple-ish image during playback
? Sometimes during playback image becomes "greenish" (sometimes:
? "purplish"). What do I do?

! The usual reason for these faults are loose connections on
! HDMI bus between the TV box and the TV.

! Try replacing the HDMI cable with something of a better quality:
! less flexible are better (although less handy), ferrite rings
! are a bonus (bulges near the ends of the cable).

! Also try inserting the cable into a different HDMI jack, if it
! is possible.

? Black screen on refresh rate switch
? Sometimes after refresh rate switching screen becomes black and
? won't restore (sometimes: will restore after video stops, or:
? restores after I reboot the box).

! This is your TV fault. Refresh rate switching is a stress situation
! for a TV - it has to determine all the parameters of the incoming
! video signal. Sometimes it fails to do it correctly, and it just
! 'hangs', although this depends from model to model.

! Some people assert that this does not happen if you switch HDMI bus
! to RGB format (normally video signal is encoded in the YUV format).

? How I can switch the HDMI bus to RGB format?
? I heard this can help with "greenish image" / "black screens" /
? bad karma etc.

! Open "Settings" -> "Other" -> "HDMI format (color space)" and choose
! "RGB instead of YUV" - YUV444 will be replaced with RGB. The "Auto" item
! returns the previous behaviour. The 4K bandwidth rule (4:2:0 is kept there)
! is preserved in every item.

! At 4k resolution and 40 Hz or above an RGB stream cannot be transmitted
! over an HDMI link - 4:2:0 is kept there in every item; that is a property
! of the link, not a setting.

! On Android 11 and above this works too: the daemon applies the selected
! format after every mode switch (through the built-in service). The exception
! is HDR/Dolby Vision: they are managed by the system and the player.

? My TV doesn't "like" certain refresh rates
? I use the "Prefer exact refresh rates" option but, still, I want to use
? 60Hz refresh instead of 30Hz. How can I do it?

! Open "Settings" -> "Mode selection" -> "Blacklisted refresh rates" and
! check 30 (and, possibly, 29.97): the list shows the rates your TV supports.
! Note that 23.976 and 24 are different rates - check the one that bothers
! you.

? At start of video display turns off even if framerate does not change.
? Why after video start the screen goes to black for some time, even if
? video framerate is not different from display refresh rate?

! On Android 6-9 AFRd turns the screen to black shortly after video start to
! avoid displaying the first frames, after which a refresh rate switch usually
! happens and is painful for eyes. This feature can be turned off in preferences.

! But at the moment when AFRd switches display to black it doesn't know yet the
! video framerate - detection takes from half to a few seconds, so an
! unnecessary black screen can't be avoided completely.

! On Android 11 and above this does not apply: the daemon does not turn the
! screen to black. It first measures the frame rate (this takes 10-20 seconds)
! and only then switches the mode, so the "click" happens 10-20 seconds after
! video start, not at the very beginning.

? Why display doesn't turn off when I start one video immediately after another?

! Preliminary switch to black takes action only if there's no active video.
! If video was already started, AFRd will avoid turning screen to black
! because it is not expected frame rate to change.

! When video ends, AFRd will wait some time before restoring original
! refresh rate. If you start a video immediately, it will be considered
! a "continuation" of previous video, thus screen won't be turned off.

! If you want display to turn off after the start of video, wait first
! for the original refresh rate to restore.

! On Android 11 and above this does not apply: the screen is never turned
! to black.

? What is the meaning of "Fractional rate usage policy" option?
? What does the option that allows to choose between "fractional" and
? "integer" refresh rates?

! Usually AFRd will correctly "guess" the video frame rate (23.976 vs 24
! and so on), but sometimes it may do it wrong.

! If you mostly watch videos with "fractional" frame rates (23.976, 29.97,
! 59.94 fps), or if you mostly watch videos with "integer" frame rates
! (24, 30, 60 fps), you may decide to set this option to avoid AFRd
! errors altogether.

! But I recommend keeping this option to "auto" - first, because AFRd
! most of the time does the right job and, second, because most likely
! you won't see the difference between a 23.976 and 24 Hz refresh,
! a slight error here will result in just a single frame inserted
! or removed every ~41 seconds.

? Why is the chosen fractional/integer rate not applied on Android 10-11?

! On these versions the mode is switched by the vendor SystemControl, and it
! rewrites the frac_rate_policy node on every mode apply. So "integer" often
! has no effect there: 23.976 matches, while exactly 24.000/60.000 run 0.1 %
! slower. AFRd says so in the log ("дробность дисплея: вендорный HAL вернул
! политику N - тайминги остаются его") and works with the actual rate. If the
! TV supports fractional modes only, "auto" always yields the fractional
! member - that is normal.

? Why refresh rate doesn't switch in my media/IPTV player?

! AFRd supports automatic frame rate switching only when the hardware decoder is
! used. Many players are able to use the software decoder - usually because of
! compatibility problems between the video and the hardware decoder. In such
! cases either use the frame rate matching built into the application (if any),
! or change its settings to use the hardware decoder.

? Do I need root (Superuser) privileges?

! Yes. AFRd switches the display mode through system mechanisms and requires
! Administrator rights - both on Android 6-9 and on Android 11 and above. The
! application asks for them on the first run (Magisk/su).

! On Android 12 and above an additional mechanism built into the firmware is
! required (SurfaceFlinger "backdoor"); Android 11 does not have it, and the
! mode is switched by the vendor SystemControl (the helper ships with AFRd2).
! If the firmware has neither, the daemon switches to the passive mode: only
! the API and statistics work, the mode is not switched.

? Should I choose the APK or the Magisk module?

! They switch the rate in exactly the same way: the same daemon is inside both
! the module and the application, and on Android 11-14 the same settings profile
! is used. The difference is the wrapper, so choose by convenience and by the
! way your box is used.

! The APK is an application with an interface: settings on screen, a status page
! (current mode, measured frame rate, statistics), the FAQ, and clear error
! messages. The application picks the daemon build matching your firmware's
! architecture and unpacks the mode-switching helper (Android 10/11) itself,
! and it watches the daemon by the liveness mark in shared memory - so it
! restarts it both when the daemon has crashed and when it has hung.

! The Magisk module is the daemon only, with no interface: settings are edited
! in the text file afrd.ini inside the module (/data/adb/modules/afrd2/). The
! daemon is started not by Android but by Magisk, so the module also starts
! frame rate matching where the application no longer can: after the
! application has been force-stopped (Android does not deliver the boot message
! to a stopped application) or after its data has been cleared. The module
! keeps the daemon supervised and restarts it after a crash (a hang it does not
! detect - the application does). The architecture (armeabi-v7a for 32-bit
! firmware or arm64-v8a for 64-bit) is chosen manually: you need the module
! matching your firmware.

! The module is preferable if:
! - the box is "set and forget": settings are edited once in afrd.ini and there
!   is nothing to show on screen;
! - the application is not needed, while its background service with a
!   permanent notification gets in the way (or the firmware vendor kills it, or
!   you "freeze" applications yourself) - without the application there is
!   neither a service nor a notification;
! - several people use the box and nobody wants or needs to change settings -
!   and frame rate matching cannot be turned off from the screen by accident;
! - frame rate matching must keep working regardless of the application state:
!   after a force-stop, after its data has been cleared, or after the APK has
!   been uninstalled;
! - you do not want an extra APK in the system: the module is installed, updated
!   and removed through Magisk, and only the module directory remains.

! The APK is preferable if:
! - you are tuning the settings for your box and players (skip lists, pause
!   tolerance, observation window, measurement calibration) - on screen this is
!   easier than editing a text file;
! - you want the status page and the FAQ at hand;
! - you prefer not to choose the architecture manually - the application picks
!   the right build itself and re-extracts the helper if the system has cleared
!   the cache;
! - you run Android 6-10: the module is claimed for Android 11-14 (it carries
!   that profile), on older versions take the APK.

! The two must not be kept at the same time: after a reboot two daemons would
! start. Before moving to the module the application must be stopped or
! disabled.

? What is frame rate "clamping" and what does the "Fix frame rate clamping" item do?

! Some players (first of all the stock YouTube on Android 11 and newer) remember
! the display rate at the very start of a video and never re-read it. If at that
! moment the mode was below the video's rate, the player keeps delivering frames
! at the old cadence even after the mode is switched: a 60 fps video started at
! 50 Hz plays at ~50 fps and stays juddery until playback is restarted. That is
! "clamping".

! AFRd2 closes it in two ways: it raises the mode to the maximum your TV supports
! before the player's first frame, and - if the switch still landed on a running
! video - resets the player's rate ceiling once: by restarting the codec process
! by default, or by bumping the display density if that fails. The player then
! re-reads the mode and delivery recovers from the current playback position.
! The reset costs a fraction of a second (measured: 0.26 s on Android 11,
! 0.18-0.25 s on Android 12-14) and works on both paths.

! The "Fix frame rate clamping" item enables both mechanisms at once. Turn it off
! only if your content always plays at the TV's maximum rate: then there is
! nothing to switch and the bump is not needed. The stock YouTube app is no
! longer excluded from matching on any version: clamping on Android 11 is closed
! by the ceiling reset (0.26 s), so keeping it in the "Players that AFR
! disturbs" list is not needed any more.

! Next to it there is the "Players to fix clamping for" list - the applications
! the mechanisms apply to (both the mode bump and the ceiling reset). By default
! it holds only the stock YouTube, the only player where clamping was measured;
! for some players the ceiling reset (a codec restart or a density change)
! breaks playback - e.g. OTT Navigator showed errors and a frozen picture when
! switching channels. Add a player to this list if you see clamping there and
! want AFRd2 to fight it. An empty list disables the mechanisms for everyone and
! leaves plain measurement. A player listed under "Players that AFR disturbs"
! never gets the mechanisms: that list promises to leave the mode alone.

! If some player is wrongly detected as 25 instead of 24 fps (or vice versa),
! that is not clamping but measurement inaccuracy: such players feed more frames
! to the decoder than the nominal rate. The correction is set per player in
! "Corrections (package=percent)", computed as (measured / nominal - 1) * 100;
! see the README section "How to calibrate the frame rate correction".
