0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 22:45:00 MSK 2026

    A caveat for switch.skip.pkg: for an excluded player the display mode is chosen
    by the system itself, so that mode must not be lower than the content rate. On
    the test panel the EDID prefers 50 Hz and the "user preferred" setting still
    held 50 Hz (leftovers from earlier experiments), so the exclusion initially
    produced 30 Hz and a 24 fps video ran at an uneven 1.25 periods. The cure is to
    set the mode explicitly: `cmd display set-user-preferred-display-mode 1920 1080 60`
    (after that both 24 and 25 fps videos play evenly at their own rate on a 60 Hz
    display). README limitation 8 updated with the caveat.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 22:30:00 MSK 2026

    New key switch.skip.pkg — players that AFR gets in the way of. For them the
    daemon measures the rate and logs everything, but never switches or holds the
    display mode (nor honours their frame rate hint). The stock YouTube is in the
    list by default.

    Reason: the player picks its render cadence at video start from the then-current
    display mode and never recovers (30 fps under 24 Hz -> 24.2 fps, 60 fps under
    50 Hz -> 50.3 fps), so a mode held from the previous video clamped the next one
    — and that is the main scenario, browsing videos. With a display the daemon
    leaves alone (usually 60 Hz) clamping is impossible in principle, and the player
    itself lays 24/25/30/60 fps out on the 60 Hz grid. As a side effect this player
    sees no screen blinking and pause.keep/switch.restore.on.stop do not apply
    (there is nothing to hold).

    mode.min.hz.list remains a tool for other clamping players (YouTube removed
    from it).

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 22:05:00 MSK 2026

    The "residual" clamping case was checked and turned out to be systematic rather
    than one-off: a 60 fps video started while a 50 Hz mode is held (i.e. right after
    a 25 or 50 fps video in the same player) is clamped to 50 fps (live measurement:
    50.35 fps with the display at 60 Hz) and does not recover by itself. Re-opening
    the video helps: afterwards 59.94 fps with all intervals at 16.7 ms.

    The daemon now names this case in the log: the cadence check threshold was
    lowered from 1.25 to 1.125 (this case — 1.18 — used to pass as "подача в
    порядке"). README limitation 8 updated.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 21:50:00 MSK 2026

    Refined the mode.min.hz.list rule: when there is no clean multiple for the
    content rate, the fastest available mode is taken instead of the one closest to
    the double. Verified live: 23.976/24 fps -> 60 Hz (at 50 Hz the hiccups are rare
    but 20 ms long, at 60 Hz the 3:2 pattern spreads them over 8 ms; the owner
    compared visually and chose 60), 25 fps -> 50 Hz (a clean multiple), 29.97/30
    fps -> 59.94 Hz.

    The source of the video on which the owner saw residual unevenness was checked
    separately: CFR 30 fps without duplicates (599 frames, all intervals 33.3 ms),
    i.e. the unevenness was on the submission side and went away after restarting
    the video — a fresh start at 60 Hz gives an even 33.4 ms.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 21:35:00 MSK 2026

    New key mode.min.hz.list ("package=Hz"): for the listed players the daemon does
    not choose modes below the given rate. This cures a "stuck render cadence": a
    player picks its render cadence at video start from the then-current display
    mode and never raises it (verified live on the stock YouTube TV: a 30 fps video
    started under a 24 Hz mode kept running at 24 fps; only restarting the video or
    the player helps). The default is com.google.android.youtube.tv=50: 24 fps
    videos on YouTube are shown at 50 Hz (mild 2:2.08 instead of an ideal 1:1), but
    30/60 fps videos after them are no longer clamped. List format is the same as
    fps.correct.list.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 21:10:00 MSK 2026

    The log now recognises "uneven frame submission". Previously the post-switch
    cadence check only complained about "exactly twice as slow" (a display-side
    half-rate defect), while a player submitting frames slower on its own — for
    example, one that picked its render cadence at video start and never raises it —
    was reported as "подача в порядке". Now, when the mean interval deviates by more
    than 25 % (and is not exactly twice), a line "подача неровная: X ms at period
    Y ms, expectation Z ms" is written. The cause was found live on the stock
    YouTube TV: a 30 fps video started under a 24 Hz mode stays at 24 fps even after
    the display is raised to 60 Hz (only restarting the video helps). A limitation
    note was added to the README.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 20:05:00 MSK 2026

    The AV1 correction is enabled with the value 0.1 — the largest one at which no
    25 fps clip is pushed into 24 Hz. Tuned on live recordings (13 YouTube clips,
    the same statistic the daemon uses — lower envelope): without the correction
    "24 correct / 25 correct" = 5/7 and 6/6; at 0.1 — 6/7 and 6/6; at 0.3 — 6/7
    and 5/6 (a genuine 25 fps clip lands in 24 Hz); at 0.5 — 7/7 and 4/6. In the
    application: "Apply player calibration" is on, "AV1 correction" = 0.1.

    No value gives a "100 %" guarantee: on a 65-clip control sample two genuine
    25 fps clips measured below the class boundary with no correction at all (the
    envelope is biased downwards), and the spread within a single playback reaches
    ±0.5 frames/s.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 19:45:00 MSK 2026

    The AV1 correction (fps.correct.*) is now off by default. The owner compared on
    a TV (all picture "enhancers" disabled): 24 fps video in a 25 Hz mode looks
    noticeably better than 25 fps in a 24 Hz mode. The correction moves the
    measurement downwards, i.e. can produce exactly the worse side, while its gain
    is small and does not reproduce from run to run. The keys remain — enable them
    deliberately, for a specific player or decoder path.

    Application settings now take effect immediately: after the config is written
    the daemon is woken up, so the "enable automatic frame rate matching" switch
    (and the notification "AFR on/off" action) apply within ~5 seconds, without
    restarting the daemon. Previously turning it off had no effect until the daemon
    was restarted.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 19:00:00 MSK 2026

    Display attributes (HDR Mode, HDR Policy, HDR Priority, SDR Mode, FORCE HDR
    Mode) are read from the built-in service and shown in the log (with -v) and

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 18:20:00 MSK 2026

    Fixed a daemon crash on config reload (the reconf command or editing
    afrd.ini): the freed pointer to the current color space was not cleared, so
    the next initialization freed the same memory again (double free, heap
    corruption). It took two reloads in a row — reproduced live on ATV14. The
    file-change check on the legacy path now uses the shared function (one rule
    instead of two copies).

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 18:10:00 MSK 2026

    Config reload (the reconf API command or editing afrd.ini) now works on
    Android 10-14 too. Previously the new path read the keys once on entry, and
    the daemon had to be restarted after editing the file.

    The daemon notices the file change and the reconf command, restores the
    original mode, releases the mode-switch flag, re-reads the config and keeps
    running — without restarting the process and without interrupting the API.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 18:00:00 MSK 2026

    The daemon no longer gets stuck on decoder leftovers and can detect the rate
    when the frame counter is silent; color space settings are applied on
    Android 10-14 again.

    If the player is closed but a decoder instance remains (the frame counter
    stands still), the daemon notices that the application has left the
    foreground and releases the mode hold — previously the mode stayed with the
    mode-switch flag set until a reboot.

    When the decoder frame counter is silent but the picture is running, the rate
    is taken from video layer submission — a source that does not depend on the
    counter.

    The color space (cs.select rules) is applied on Android 10-14 as well: the
    value is set through the built-in service after a mode switch.

0.3.3 Fri Oct 02 12:00:00 MSK 2026

    The application and the daemon are now called AFRd2.

    Previously the mode was switched only after twenty seconds of observation. Now,
    when two independent rate measurements agree, the switch happens earlier.

    After switching, the daemon checks that frames really reach the display and, on
    a "half rate" picture (video looks like thirteen frames per second), switches
    the mode once more.

    If the daemon stops working, the application starts it again; the stuck
    mode-switch flag is released, and applications with their own frame rate
    matching can change the mode again.

    The active decoder channel is chosen by the growing frame counter, so a stuck
    channel no longer prevents rate detection.

0.3.2 Tue Sep 29 2026

    AFRd was forked: support for Android 10-14 added, the old path (Android 6-9)
    is unchanged.

    On newer kernels the decoder does not report video start and stop, so the
    daemon polls the decoder state itself and switches the mode through the stock
    SurfaceFlinger mechanism.

    The rate is detected from the decoder frame counter (lower envelope over
    sub-windows), with a correction for AV1.
